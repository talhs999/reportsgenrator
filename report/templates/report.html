Documentation & Identification Review
Section 2 — Document Verification

A thorough documentation review is an essential step in assessing the provenance and legitimacy of any used vehicle. The following key documents and identifiers should be verified before completing a purchase.

V5C Registration Certificate (Logbook)

The V5C is the most important document associated with a vehicle. It records the registered keeper (not necessarily the legal owner) and contains key details including the VIN, engine number, colour, and date of first registration. The current V5C format has multiple security features including a watermark, colour-coded sections, and a unique document reference number.

Key checks: Ensure the V5C document reference number matches DVLA records, the registered keeper details match the seller, and there are no signs of tampering or alteration. A vehicle without a V5C should be treated with extreme caution.

Vehicle Identification Number (VIN)

The VIN is a unique 17-character code assigned during manufacture. It can typically be found on the dashboard (visible through the windscreen), on a plate in the driver's door frame, and stamped into the chassis. The VIN on the vehicle must match the VIN recorded on the V5C and the DVLA database. Any discrepancy may indicate the vehicle has been cloned or its identity altered.

Service History

A full service history demonstrates that the vehicle has been maintained according to the manufacturer's recommended schedule. Service records may be in the form of a stamped service book, digital service records (maintained by the manufacturer's dealer network), or individual invoices from independent garages. Gaps in service history may indicate periods of neglected maintenance.

All vehicles over 3 years old in the UK require an annual MOT test to ensure they meet the minimum road safety and environmental standards set by the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA). Driving without a valid MOT is an offence under the Road Traffic Act 1988 and can result in a fine of up to £1,000.

Vehicle Excise Duty (road tax) must be paid for all vehicles used or kept on public roads. The tax rate is determined by the vehicle's CO2 emissions (for vehicles registered after April 2017) or engine size (for older vehicles). Untaxed vehicles on public roads may be clamped, impounded, or destroyed.
